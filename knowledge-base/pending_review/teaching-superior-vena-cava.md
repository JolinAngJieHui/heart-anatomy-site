## superior-vena-cava-1: anatomy

Pattern: normal; relationship: direct; structures: superior-vena-cava; levels: basic, intermediate, advanced.

The right and left brachiocephalic veins unite to form the SVC, which enters the right atrium on the right side of the heart.

Evidence: [cc-vena-cava: Vena Cava](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22619-vena-cava) — Anatomy — Where is the vena cava located?. Cleveland Clinic Health Library; medically reviewed; updated 2022-03-24. Registry source version: 1. Access: readable-page.

## superior-vena-cava-2: physiology

Pattern: normal; relationship: direct; structures: superior-vena-cava; levels: basic, intermediate, advanced.

The SVC returns oxygen-poor blood from the upper body to the right atrium, supporting venous return rather than myocardial arterial supply.

Evidence: [cc-vena-cava: Vena Cava](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22619-vena-cava) — Function — What does the vena cava do?. Cleveland Clinic Health Library; medically reviewed; updated 2022-03-24. Registry source version: 1. Access: readable-page.

## superior-vena-cava-3: clinical

Pattern: clinical-context; relationship: direct; structures: superior-vena-cava; levels: intermediate, advanced.

SVC obstruction from compression or thrombosis can impair upper-body venous return. Catheters and pacemakers are relevant clot-associated contexts; swelling and breathlessness are possible consequences.

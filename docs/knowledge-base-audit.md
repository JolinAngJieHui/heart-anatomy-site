## Access and metadata findings

The 23 supplied references include seven Cleveland Clinic pages, Radiology Assistant, Mayo Clinic and the ASCI consensus with readable source pages. Six other PMC articles and four Bookshelf chapters encounter direct-page browser challenges; alternate primary indexing/PubMed records support identity, but these challenges do not establish a broken URL or permission to import text. ScienceDirect Topics returns forbidden access; its individual authors and publication details remain unverified. The AHA DOI endpoint is blocked. PubMed 2022049 has limited direct rendering; its older clinical study must not become current universal guidance. No confirmed wrong-article URL was established; inaccessible content remains unresolved, not declared verified full text.

The ASCI article author list omitted Sang Il Choi, Hwan Seok Yong and Sung Mok Kim. Corrected both the registry and registration script from the original article. No source was approved. Its segmentation agreement is not a universal anatomical rule. The CT segmentation/tracking paper is a methods reference, the Ilkay ECG classification a selected-cohort clinical study, and the anomalous LCx and combined right-sinus ostia reports variant cases, not normal-anatomy atlases.

The draft registry is a separate candidate inventory, including the 17 website explanations and older external links. Those explanations are not academically approved. Overlapping URLs across draft and main registries are not additional independent evidence. Some draft sources (dominance review, SVC chapter and dedicated LAD chapter) could improve coverage after review, rights clearance and extraction. They are not production retrieval inputs.

## Scientific review priorities

- Reconcile proximal/mid/distal RCA and LAD boundaries under an explicit scheme. The website's apical LAD label needs its own crosswalk; a distal segment illustration does not prove apical equivalence.
- Add dedicated D2, acute marginal and conus normal anatomy, variable territories and collateral coverage. Figure 3A in the coronary-anomalies review does not itself illustrate every website branch; use the relevant normal-anatomy text and verify each locator.
- Clarify generic OM versus OM1/OM2 and left main versus the complete left coronary tree.
- Reconcile dominance descriptions across consumer and specialist sources, including the flagged Cleveland Clinic LAD wording. PDA origin depends on dominance; the site's right-sided grouping is illustrative.
- Add specialist ascending-aorta/root, SVC relationships/variants and coronary-sinus ostium/valves/tributaries/procedural material. Normal dominance and separate conus ostia must be distinguished from rare combined anomalies.
- Basic/Intermediate broad descriptions and Advanced consensus/clinical/variant context are candidate educational uses. No level currently has approved retrievable evidence; detailed physiology and clinical completeness remain unestablished.

## Access, reuse and readiness

Public readability is not a reuse grant. Cleveland Clinic and Mayo have restrictive copying terms. PMC licences are article-specific and do not automatically cover third-party figures. NC/ND terms, unverified rights, ScienceDirect excerpts and AHA restrictions need case-specific clearance before extraction or external indexing. No full texts or figures were copied in this audit, and no external indexing was performed.

Only one pending attributed summary is locally staged. Registration is not extraction. Before real-reference retrieval testing, a reviewer must approve suitable exact text, its scientific content, source metadata, version/hash and applicable reuse/indexing rights. The architecture is ready for isolated local technical testing; the real-reference knowledge base is not yet ready for evidence-based retrieval.

## Deduplicated source register and reading targets

The target descriptions below guide manual extraction; they are not guaranteed exact parser selectors. ### R1 — Coronary Artery Anomalies: A Computed Tomography Angiography Pictorial Review

Baz RO, Refi D, Scheau C, Savulescu-Fiedler I, Baz RA, Niscoveanu C (2024). *Journal of Clinical Medicine*, 13(13):3920. DOI: [10.3390/jcm13133920](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm13133920).

**Type:** Pictorial review (use text only). **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382016251_Coronary_Artery_Anomalies_A_Computed_Tomography_Angiography_Pictorial_Review).

**Start with:** Section 3: Anatomy of the Coronary Arteries — selected normal-anatomy paragraphs.

**Scope:** Shared normal coronary origins, courses, branches, segments, territories and dominance.

**Review boundaries:** Do not ingest figure images, captions, or anomaly sections by default. Exclude the left-main-length statement of 2–4 mm pending scientific review; R3 is the dedicated left-main source. Do not treat apical LAD as an automatic synonym for a standardized distal segment. D1/D2-specific clinical diagnosis is not established by this anatomy review.

### R2 — Right ventricular myocardial infarction: pathophysiology, clinical implications and management

Femia G, French JK, Juergens C, Leung D, Lo S (2021). *Reviews in Cardiovascular Medicine*, 22(4):1229–1240. DOI: [10.31083/j.rcm2204131](https://doi.org/10.31083/j.rcm2204131).

**Type:** Review. **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/RCM/22/4/10.31083/j.rcm2204131).

**Start with:** 1. Introduction; 2. Right ventricular anatomy, function and arterial blood supply; 3. Pathophysiology of right ventricular myocardial infarction; 4. Diagnosis — clinical presentation and non-invasive diagnostic explanations.

**Scope:** RCA and right-ventricular branch anatomy, occlusion consequences, ECG, echocardiography and other diagnostic modalities.

**Review boundaries:** Right-ventricular infarction is not the inevitable consequence of every RCA/PDA lesion. PDA diagnostic coverage is shared inferior-infarction/RCA context, not an isolated-PDA algorithm. Do not use treatment regimens, invasive-procedure instructions or unreviewed numerical diagnostic cutoffs in the starter tutor.

### R3 — Left Main Coronary Artery Disease: A Contemporary Review of Diagnosis and Management

Khawaja M, Britt M, Khan MA, et al. (2024). *Reviews in Cardiovascular Medicine*, 25(2):66. DOI: [10.31083/j.rcm2502066](https://doi.org/10.31083/j.rcm2502066).

**Type:** Review. **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/RCM/25/2/10.31083/j.rcm2502066).

**Start with:** Introduction and clinical presentation; 3. Diagnosis of Left Main Disease; Anatomy-related passages in 4. LMCAD Pathophysiology and Genetic/Phenotypic Considerations.

**Scope:** Left-main origin and branching, significance of obstruction, clinical presentation, ECG, echo, coronary CT and invasive assessment.

**Review boundaries:** Map the website label Left Coronary Artery to the left-main trunk, not the entire left coronary arterial tree. Exclude intervention-selection rules and drug regimens from the basic anatomy tutor.

### R4 — Clinical Updates in Coronary Artery Disease: A Comprehensive Review

Bottardi A, Prado GFA, Lunardi M, Fezzi S, Pesarini G, Tavella D, Scarsini R, Ribichini F (2024). *Journal of Clinical Medicine*, 13(16):4600. DOI: [10.3390/jcm13164600](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm13164600).

**Type:** Review. **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382925090_Clinical_Updates_in_Coronary_Artery_Disease_A_Comprehensive_Review).

**Start with:** 1. Introduction; 2. Updates in CAD Diagnosis: The Role of CCTA, especially 2.1. Traditional CAD Diagnostic Tests.

**Scope:** General coronary artery disease, acute/chronic presentations and diagnostic test categories; shared clinical context for the LAD.

**Review boundaries:** This is not an LAD-segment-specific diagnostic reference. Do not infer a unique ECG pattern or prognosis for proximal, mid or apical LAD from general CAD content. Exclude treatment and device sections from the starter extraction.

### R5 — The diagonal branches and outcomes in patients with anterior ST-elevation myocardial infarction

Zhang S, Deng X, Yang W, et al. (2020). *BMC Cardiovascular Disorders*, 20:108. DOI: [10.1186/s12872-020-01386-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12872-020-01386-4).

**Type:** Retrospective observational study. **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12872-020-01386-4).

**Start with:** Background; Discussion — anatomical variability, diagonal-flow loss and clinical interpretation; Limitations.

**Scope:** Diagonal branch anatomy and variable territory; relationship between diagonal flow, anterior infarction and angiographic assessment.

**Review boundaries:** The cohort concerns anterior STEMI and main-diagonal flow, commonly the first diagonal; it is not a dedicated isolated-D2 study. Do not transfer cohort outcome rates to every D1 or D2 occlusion, or make causal claims from these associations. Do not import study eligibility criteria as universal diagnostic criteria.

**Type:** Review. **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/RCM/27/2/10.31083/RCM47426).

### R7 — Successful coronary angioplasty of a spontaneous occlusion of the conus artery causing a ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction and electrical storm

**Type:** Case report. **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42269-024-01247-6).

**Start with:** Background; Selected non-identifying diagnostic narrative and discussion.

**Scope:** A documented example of conus occlusion, myocardial injury/arrhythmia, ECG findings and angiographic confirmation.

### R8 — Imaging of the aortic root on high-pitch non-gated and ECG-gated CT: awareness is the key!

Nagpal P, Agrawal MD, Saboo SS, Hedgire S, Priya S, Steigner ML (2020). *Insights into Imaging*, 11:51. DOI: [10.1186/s13244-020-00855-w](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13244-020-00855-w).

**Type:** Imaging review. **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13244-020-00855-w).

**Start with:** Aortic anatomy—aortic root and ascending aorta; Lesions of the proximal aorta — selected aneurysm and dissection explanations.

**Scope:** Aortic-root/ascending-aorta anatomy, aneurysm, dissection and diagnostic imaging.

### R9 — Optimal Management of Spontaneous Aortic Thrombus Floating in the Ascending Aorta, from a Single Case Experience to a Literature Review

Gardellini J, Linardi D, Di Nicola V, Puntel G, Puppini G, Barozzi L, Luciani GB (2025). *Journal of Cardiovascular Development and Disease*, 12(4):146. DOI: [10.3390/jcdd12040146](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcdd12040146).

**Type:** Case report with literature review. **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390648109_Optimal_Management_of_Spontaneous_Aortic_Thrombus_Floating_in_the_Ascending_Aorta_from_a_Single_Case_Experience_to_a_Literature_Review).

**Start with:** Introduction; Discussion — embolic consequences and diagnostic imaging.

**Scope:** Ascending-aortic thrombus, downstream embolic complications, CT angiography and echocardiography.

**Review boundaries:** A floating thrombus can cause downstream embolism without completely blocking the ascending aorta. Evidence is substantially case-based; do not turn management discussion into universal treatment rules. Exclude patient images, procedural details and drug regimens.

Straka C, Ying J, Kong FM, Willey CD, Kaminski J, Kim DWN (2016). *SpringerPlus*, 5:229. DOI: [10.1186/s40064-016-1900-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40064-016-1900-7).

### R11 — Coronary sinus thrombosis: insights from a comprehensive literature review

Yi C (2025). *Thrombosis Journal*, 23:24. DOI: [10.1186/s12959-025-00707-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12959-025-00707-x).

**Type:** Review. **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Licence evidence / original paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12959-025-00707-x).

**Start with:** Anatomy/function discussion; Pathophysiology of coronary sinus thrombosis; Clinical presentation and diagnosis discussion.

**Scope:** Coronary venous drainage, sinus thrombosis, consequences and diagnosis by echocardiography and cross-sectional imaging.

**Review boundaries:** Distinguish venous outflow obstruction from coronary arterial inflow obstruction. Do not present a definitive diagnosis from a nonspecific ECG finding. Exclude treatment regimens and unreviewed numerical claims.

## Text-only RAG conditions and launch boundaries

- Use only the article authors’ text covered by the stated licence; exclude separately credited third-party material unless separately cleared.
- Retain author(s), title, publication details, DOI/source link, copyright notice when supplied, CC BY 4.0 label and licence link.
- Mark extraction/formatting changes and identify AI-generated explanations as generated paraphrases rather than author quotations or endorsement.
- Exclude images, figures, figure captions and supplementary files from the starter upload. Keep tables out until separately reviewed.
- Upload each unique selected source/extract once; map its source ID to multiple vessel labels instead of duplicating the text.
- Retain section locators and original reference markers in extracted prose. Do not promote articles merely cited by a review into independently reviewed sources.
- Allow cross-vessel retrieval when relevant; selected structure should guide ranking, not force irrelevant or unsupported answers.
- For unsupported branch-specific diagnosis, exact segment boundaries, treatment or prognosis, state that the curated material does not establish the answer.
- This register does not alter the existing knowledge base, grant scientific approval or perform any upload or paid API call.

## Suggested attribution metadata

Retain: `source_id`, `authors`, `title`, `year`, `journal`, `doi`, `source_url`, `licence`, `licence_url`, `copyright_notice`, `section_heading`, `extraction_date`, `changes_notice`, and `structure_ids`.

Example change notice: “Article text excerpted and formatting normalised for retrieval; images and supplements omitted. Student-facing explanations are AI-generated paraphrases and are not endorsed by the original authors.”

For the first test, restrict answers to reviewed normal anatomy, broad consequences of impaired flow and explanations of diagnostic tests. Do not ask the tutor to diagnose a student/patient or generate treatment instructions.

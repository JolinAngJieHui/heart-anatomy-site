## coronary-sinus-1: anatomy

Pattern: normal; relationship: direct; structures: coronary-sinus; levels: basic, intermediate, advanced.

The coronary sinus lies in the left posterior atrioventricular groove and opens into the right atrium. Tributaries include great, middle and small cardiac veins, posterior left ventricular and oblique atrial veins.

Evidence: [nbk549786: Anatomy, Thorax, Heart Veins](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549786/) — Structure and Function — Coronary Sinus. StatPearls [Internet], StatPearls Publishing; updated 2023-07-24; current container 2026 Jan. Registry source version: 1. Access: primary-search-excerpt.

## coronary-sinus-2: physiology

Pattern: normal; relationship: direct; structures: coronary-sinus; levels: basic, intermediate, advanced.

The coronary sinus returns myocardial venous blood to the right atrium. It is part of cardiac venous drainage, distinct from the systemic return through SVC.

Evidence: [nbk557566: Anatomy, Thorax, Coronary Sinus](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557566/) — Introduction; Structure and Function. StatPearls [Internet], StatPearls Publishing; updated 2023-07-24; current container 2026 Jan. Registry source version: 1. Access: primary-search-excerpt.

## coronary-sinus-3: clinical

Pattern: clinical-context; relationship: direct; structures: coronary-sinus; levels: advanced.

Coronary venous anatomy is relevant to resynchronization lead access, retrograde cardioplegia and electrophysiologic mapping. Valves and tributary variation can complicate access; detailed technique is beyond these notes.
